Models, not settings

This page is about what the elements of ReMoto are. If you want to know in which order they run and how a signal travels between them, that is the companion page, inside a simulation. If you want to get something running, start with the quick guide.

Two things are worth knowing before the list. First, almost everything starts switched off: a scenario creates only what it declares, and a model that is not in the scenario is not simulated at all — not simulated as zero. Second, several elements come in more than one model, and the scenario chooses: the muscle, the spindle, the joint, and now the motoneuron membrane. The choice the old site made is still the default everywhere, so published scenarios keep giving published results.

Every parameter named here is editable, and the editor keeps the screens, names, units and limits of the original site — which is why the papers written for it still describe what you see.

1Membranes

The motoneuron, and the interneuron

A motoneuron has two compartments, a soma and a dendrite, each with its own potential, joined by a coupling conductance. Potentials are measured from rest and held between −16 and 120 mV.

The passive properties are not typed in directly: they are computed from the geometry. Each compartment has a diameter and a length, which give an area; with the specific membrane capacitance and the specific membrane resistance, the area gives the capacitance and the leak conductance; with the cytoplasm resistivity, the two geometries give the coupling conductance between them. Changing a diameter therefore changes the input resistance, the time constant and the rheobase at once, as it would in a cell.

On top of the passive membrane sit the active conductances. In the soma, a sodium channel that activates and inactivates, a fast potassium channel and a slow one; the slow one is what sets the afterhyperpolarisation, and therefore most of the firing rate. In the dendrite, a calcium conductance that carries the persistent inward current, scaled by a gain usually written γ: at zero the dendrite is passive, and the pool behaves as in the model published in 2008; above zero it has the active dendrite of the later work.

There are two formulations of those channels, and the scenario chooses on the motoneuron screen. In the original one the rate constants are swapped for a pulse of fixed duration when the soma crosses threshold, which is cheap and reproduces the spike shape by construction; it is the default. In the continuous one the gating variables are integrated on every step, Hodgkin-Huxley style, with a persistent sodium current added in the soma and an inactivating L-type calcium current in the dendrite. The practical difference: in the first, the persistent inward current is an event; in the second it is a state of the membrane, which is what produces plateau potentials and self-sustained firing.

A pool is not uniform. Within a nucleus every parameter is graded from the first neuron to the last, and the pool is divided into types — S, FR and FF — each with its own range. The small, high-resistance, low-threshold cells come in first and the large ones last, which is the size principle: it is not imposed anywhere in the code, it falls out of the gradient.

Interneurons have the somatic compartment only, with the same channels and their own refractory period. There are four kinds in the model: Renshaw cells, Ia inhibitory interneurons, and the interneurons of the Ib and group II pathways.

2Synapses

Conductance, not current

Each compartment has one excitatory and one inhibitory group of synapses. A group is not a list of independent synapses being integrated one by one — it is a single conductance that all of them contribute to, which is what makes a nucleus with hundreds of thousands of connections tractable.

A presynaptic spike arrives after the delay of its own synapse and releases transmitter for a pulse of fixed duration. During the pulse the bound fraction of receptor rises towards a saturating value with one time constant; after it, it decays with another. This is the kinetic scheme of Destexhe and colleagues, and the saturation matters: two spikes close together do not deliver twice as much as one.

The group keeps two running sums — the synapses currently in a pulse, and the rest — each with a closed-form solution between events, in the way proposed by Lytton. The conductance is the maximal conductance of the group times those sums, and the current is that conductance against the membrane potential of the moment.

Two refinements are available. A synapse may show depression or facilitation: its maximal conductance changes by a fixed percentage at each spike and recovers exponentially, so a train does not deliver the same thing throughout. And the weight of a connection may fall with the distance between the two cells along the nucleus, which keeps recurrent inhibition local; in the original this existed only between Renshaw cells and motoneurons, and here it can be set for any pathway.

3Drive

Descending tracts and noise

A descending tract is a population of axons that fire as stochastic point processes. They have no membrane: they are sources of spikes, with statistics the scenario chooses.

Three kinds are available: Poisson, a gamma process of integer order, and Gaussian intervals. The order of the gamma process is the knob that matters most and the easiest to overlook: it controls how regular the train is, and a regular command and an irregular one of the same mean rate give visibly different force variability. Independent noise sources use the same machinery.

The mean interval, or the mean rate, can follow a modulating signal: constant, sine, amplitude-modulated sine, square, ramp, triangle, trapezoid, random, or an arbitrary series of points. Injected currents and nerve stimuli draw on the same family of signals, so a ramp of current and a ramp of descending drive are described the same way.

4Axons and nerve

Conduction, collision and stimulation

Each motoneuron and each afferent fibre has a conduction velocity, graded across the pool, and from it come the latencies between the spinal cord, the stimulation site and the end plate.

Spikes travel in both directions. One leaving the soma goes to the muscle; one raised by a nerve stimulus in the middle of the axon goes both ways, towards the muscle, giving the M wave, and back towards the soma. Two spikes meeting on the same axon annihilate — that collision is why the H reflex shrinks as the stimulus grows, and it is modelled rather than drawn.

How a stimulus decides which axons it excites has two models. The original compares the stimulus current with a threshold for each fibre. The alternative, from the Fortran version, models the node of Ranvier: a human axon model with its channels, pumps and the internode, with motor and sensory parameters from Howells and colleagues (2012). With it the pulse width matters as it does in an experiment — there is a strength-duration relation, around 0.4 ms in the motor fibre and 0.5 ms in the sensory one — and the node is refractory, so paired pulses behave properly.

A pool may also have a fraction of its axons blocked, counted from the slowest. Those motoneurons fire at the soma and appear in the raster, but nothing reaches the end plate: it is the conduction block of demyelinating disease, and it comes from the Fortran version adapted for them.

5Receptors

The spindle and the tendon organ

The two receptors differ in what they measure, in how elaborate they are and in how much of them you can control.

The muscle spindle is the detailed one. The default model, after Mileusnic and colleagues, has three intrafusal fibres — a dynamic bag, a static bag and a chain — each with its own mechanics and its own tension, and the primary (Ia) and secondary (II) afferent activities are formed from them with their own gains. Its sensitivity is itself under control, through the fusimotor drive: a dynamic and a static gamma input, which the scenario can hold constant or vary over time. A lumped alternative, named after Prochazka in the code, replaces the whole thing with a direct relation from length and velocity to firing rate — much cheaper, and enough when the spindle is an input rather than an object of study.

The Golgi tendon organ is lumped in both senses: one model, named after Lin and Crago in the code, turning the force in the tendon into the Ib afferent rate. It is a tension sensor, so what it reports is what the muscle is actually pulling with — which is not the same as how hard the pool is being driven, and the difference is most of what makes Ib feedback interesting.

Both receptors reach the circuit the same way: the afferent fibre fires as a gamma process whose rate follows the receptor, conducts with its own velocity, and lands on synapses of motoneurons and interneurons. The order of that gamma process was fixed in the original code and is editable here.

A practical consequence of how the defaults are wired: with the critically damped muscle and the isometric ankle, the spindle is not integrated at all. If you want spindle output, the scenario needs a muscle model that provides length and velocity.

6Motor units

One spike, a twitch and a potential

A motor unit is the bridge between the two halves of the model: it takes spikes and produces one mechanical and one electrical signal, computed separately all the way to the end.

The twitch is an alpha function with its own peak amplitude and time to peak, advanced as a second-order system. Slow units have small, long twitches and fast ones large, brief ones; a unit firing repeatedly sums its own twitches, which is why force fuses as rate climbs.

The action potential is a Hermite-Rodriguez waveform with its own amplitude and time constant, attenuated according to where the unit sits in the cross-section of the muscle. Units are distributed across that section, and a deep unit is seen faintly by the electrode while a superficial one dominates. The distribution itself is a scenario choice.

Both properties are graded along the pool, like everything else, and both are available per unit in the results, together with the position that set the attenuation.

7Muscle

Three ways to make force, and the EMG

The twitches have to become a muscle force, and there is more than one model for that. The scenario chooses on the biomechanics screen.

  1. Second-order critically damped system

    The default, and the one the published scenarios use. Each unit contributes its twitch and the muscle force is the sum. There is no length or velocity dependence: it is the right choice when the question is about the pool and not about the mechanics.

  2. Hill-type muscle with tendon

    The units drive an activation, separately for type I and type II units, and the activation drives a contractile element with its force-length and force-velocity properties, at a pennation angle, in series with an elastic tendon. Force now depends on how long the muscle is and how fast it is changing. This is the model to use when the joint moves, and the one that gives the spindle and the tendon organ something to read.

  3. Raikova

    Another twitch model, named after Raikova in the code, without Hill's mechanics. It is an alternative shape for the twitch, not a different muscle.

A fourth option, distribution moments, exists in the editor and is kept for compatibility with the old database, but it never computed force: it stays at zero. It is listed here so that a flat force curve with that model selected is recognised for what it is.

The EMG is a separate path with the same spikes. The raw signal is the sum of the action potentials of every unit, with the attenuation of each. What the results screen shows by default is that sum with noise added and a band pass from 50 to 1000 Hz, which is how the old site presented it; the filter order, the cut-offs, the noise level, the attenuation and the sampling are all options, and the unfiltered sum is available, as is the EMG of a single stored unit.

8Geometry and joint

From force to torque, and back to length

What connects the muscles to the joint, in both directions, is the musculotendon geometry: polynomials that give the length of each muscle and its moment arm as functions of the ankle angle, with the knee angle taken into account for the muscles that cross it.

They work both ways on every step. The angle gives each muscle its current length, which is what the spindle reads; the force times the moment arm gives the torque the joint receives. Four muscles are modelled — soleus, medial and lateral gastrocnemius, tibialis anterior — so the ankle has a plantar flexor group and a dorsiflexor antagonist.

  1. Isometric

    The default. The angle does not change, muscle lengths are fixed, and the loop through the spindle stays quiet unless something else stretches the muscle. The right choice for studying recruitment, firing statistics and force under a steady command.

  2. Inverted pendulum

    The model of upright stance: a body above the ankle, with its inertia, swaying under gravity. The sway stretches the muscles, the spindles report it, the pool reacts and the torque changes the sway. The full loop, with gravity as the disturbance.

  3. Position task

    The joint moves against a load, as in an experiment where the subject holds a position rather than a force.

  4. Imposed angle

    Not in the original site, and the simplest way to deliver a perturbation: the scenario drives the angle along a signal of its own — a tendon tap, a ramp stretch — and everything downstream responds freely.

9Defaults

What is fixed, and what you can swap

The configurations that came from the old database carry one fixed set of models: the critically damped muscle, the isometric ankle, the Mileusnic spindle and the lumped tendon organ. That is what the original data store could record, and it is why those scenarios reproduce exactly.

Everything else is a choice the editor can make and the scenario file keeps alongside the configuration: the muscle model, the joint model, the spindle model, the EMG model, the motoneuron membrane, the distribution of units in the cross-section, the fusimotor gains and the imposed angle. A scenario that does not choose gets the original behaviour, which is the rule that keeps the 45 public scenarios reproducible while the model grows.

Where the parameters live, screen by screen, is in the quick guide; what each public scenario switches on is in the scenario list; and the order in which all of this runs is in inside a simulation.
