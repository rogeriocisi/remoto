Reading the list, and this page

Every scenario is a file: the whole configuration of a simulation, its duration, its integration step and its random seed. The 45 below are public and read-only; Run starts one as it is, and Edit makes a copy of it under My scenarios and opens the editor on the copy, so nothing you do here can spoil them for anyone else.

Each entry gives the name as it appears in the simulator, the simulated duration and the file name (the scenario's id, which is also what the command line takes: scenarios/<id>.json). The durations matter: the models are detailed, and one simulated second takes something like 17 to 30 seconds of real time on a four-core machine, so a 50 ms scenario answers at once and a 30 s one is a coffee break.

Where the scenarios come from is part of what they are:

remoto-NN
Configurations of the original database, exported as they were recorded, with the number they had. They are the simulations of the old site, the ones the published papers used.
projectFR
The examples of projectFR, the Fortran version of ReMoto: the design of each experiment is the one of the example, with the model parameters of ReMoto in Java on top of configuration 24.
ReMoto_AIDP
The force and position tasks of ReMoto_AIDP, the Fortran version for demyelinating diseases, with its three subjects.
The others
Ankle stretch reflex, Full model and the two scenarios of the IA880 assignment were built for this version, to show features the older ones do not use.

One warning that saves time: almost everything in the model starts switched off, and a scenario only creates what it declares. If a plot is flat, the usual reason is that the element is not in the scenario — afferents with no spindle to drive them, interneurons with no afferent input, a joint that cannot move. The description of each scenario says what is on.

1Contraction

Contraction and recruitment

A motor nucleus driven by a descending command or by an injected current, with the ankle held still: force, EMG, spikes and the order in which the units come in.

  1. Default – Soleus contraction 5 s · remoto-17-default-soleus-contraction

    The default scenario of the old site, and the shortest way into everything else: the 900 motoneurons of the soleus receive 100 descending axons firing as Poisson processes with a mean interval of 3 ms, with 30% connectivity, and nothing else is on.

    Look at the force of the SOL, its EMG, the spike raster, and the interval histogram or the firing rate of a single motoneuron. Change the mean interval (Structure → Descending Commands) and the connectivity (Neural Parameters → Synapses) to see how the statistics of the command reach the force.

  2. Isometric contraction 5 s · remoto-14-isometric-contraction

    The whole triceps surae: soleus, medial and lateral gastrocnemius, with their own pools of S, FR and FF motoneurons, driven by 400 descending axons with a mean interval of 11 ms. No afferents and no interneurons.

    Look at the force, the EMG and the torque of each muscle side by side: the three nuclei share the command but differ in size and in the properties of their units.

  3. Recruitment 20 s · remoto-16-recruitment

    The same triceps surae, with a command that grows: the mean interval of the 400 descending axons falls as a ramp from 20 ms to 4 ms over the first 16 s and stays there. As the command grows, more motoneurons are recruited and the ones already firing go faster.

    Look at the raster of the SOL beside the force: new units appear from the bottom up, in the order of their thresholds, and the force rises in both ways at once. It is the size principle in one figure.

  4. Demo 2 – Ramp activation of Medial Gastrocnemius 6 s · demo-2-ramp-mg

    Recruitment without any synapse: the same current is injected into the soma of every motoneuron of the medial gastrocnemius (300 S, 150 FR, 150 FF), rising from 0 to 20 nA in the first 4 s and staying there until the end. With the peak at 20 nA, 377 of the 600 motoneurons fire and the force settles near 980 N; at 8 nA, 110 fire and the force stays around 74 N.

    Look at the firing rate of MN S 1, which rises while the current rises and becomes constant when the ramp reaches its peak, and at its injected current, which the scenario stores. Change the peak in Stimulation → Injected Current (field Peak, one row per type). It is the first task of the IA880 assignment.

  5. variability_gamma 5 s · remoto-32-variability-gamma

    An isometric contraction of the triceps surae with a strong command (mean interval of 3 ms), recorded for a study of force variability with Poisson and gamma commands. The order of the gamma process was not in the database, so the axons fire in Poisson here.

    Change the distribution to Gamma and set the order (field Std, in Descending Commands) to get the comparison the original study made: with the same mean rate, a more regular command gives a steadier force.

  6. IsometricTaskAM 8 s · remoto-39-isometrictaskam

    The triceps surae with 400 descending axons (mean interval of 15 ms) and an excitatory synaptic noise, with the Ia afferents of the three muscles declared. Despite the name, the command is not modulated.

    A good example of the warning above: with the isometric ankle and the default muscle the spindle is not integrated, so the Ia afferents are there and never fire. To wake them up, choose the Hill muscle and a joint that moves (Biomechanical).

  7. projectFR – Motor-unit pool with descending command 1 s · projectfr-descending-command

    The 900 motoneurons of the soleus driven by 400 descending axons at 60 spikes/s in a gamma process of order 10 — a much more regular command than Poisson.

    Look at the EMG of the pool and the force in one second of contraction. The original example also draws the EMG of each of the first 20 units, which is in the results here as the raw EMG of a chosen motor unit.

  8. projectFR – Motor-unit pool EMG 5 s · projectfr-motor-unit-pool-emg

    800 S motor units of the soleus with no synaptic input at all: each motoneuron gets a constant current of 20 nA in the dendrite, and the spikes give the force and the EMG.

    Look at the EMG and at the properties table of the motor units: with no synapses in the way, the surface EMG is the plain sum of the action potentials of units that differ only in size, depth and rate.

  9. projectFR – Force variability 16 s · projectfr-force-variability

    The variability of the isometric force of the triceps surae: 400 descending axons in a gamma process of order 4 at 140 spikes/s, with the ankle isometric. It is one of the combinations of an example that ran nine rates in a batch.

    Look at the statistics of the force (mean, standard deviation and coefficient of variation) under Analysis, in the second half of the simulation. For the whole batch, on the command line: remoto sweep --protocol force --tract CM_ext.

2Reflexes

Reflexes and nerve stimulation

What a pulse on a nerve, or a stretch, provokes back: the M wave, the H reflex, reciprocal inhibition and the stretch reflex.

  1. projectFR – H-reflex and M-wave curve 50 ms · projectfr-h-reflex-m-wave

    The classic recruitment experiment, and the fastest scenario of all: a 1 ms pulse of 12 mA on the posterior tibial nerve at 5 ms excites the motor axons of the soleus (the M wave, a few ms later) and its Ia afferents, which excite the motoneurons back (the H reflex, about 30 ms later). At 12 mA the pulse passes the threshold of part of the afferents and of almost no motor axon.

    Look at the EMG of the SOL: the M wave and the H reflex in one trace. Change the intensity (Stimulation → Nerve Stimulation) and the whole curve appears, one run at a time; on the command line the batch is one command: remoto sweep --protocol hm --range 9:25:0.5. It is the third task of the IA880 assignment.

  2. IA880 – Conditioned H reflex (reciprocal inhibition) 100 ms · ia880-h-reflex-conditioning

    The same H reflex, now with the reciprocal inhibition pathway in place: besides the test pulse on the posterior tibial nerve, a conditioning pulse on the common peroneal nerve recruits the Ia afferents of the tibialis anterior, which excite 350 reciprocal Ia interneurons, which in turn inhibit the motoneurons of the soleus.

    Look at the H reflex in the EMG of the SOL and at the spikes of the interneurons. Change the instant of the conditioning pulse (Stimulation → Nerve Stimulation, field Start of the CPN) and the inhibition appears and disappears: the conditioning–test interval is what the fourth task of the IA880 assignment asks for. On the command line: remoto sweep --protocol ct --range 0:20:1.

  3. Neuroscience Meeting 2012 200 ms · remoto-18-neuroscience-meeting-2012

    The H reflex at a supramaximal intensity: a 50 mA pulse on the posterior tibial nerve at 100 ms, with the whole reciprocal pathway declared (400 Ia afferents of the soleus, 280 of the tibialis anterior and 350 interneurons) and a pulse on the common peroneal nerve at 97 ms whose intensity, as recorded, is zero.

    Look at the EMG: at 50 mA every motor axon fires, the M wave is maximal and the H reflex is gone, collided with by the antidromic spikes. Change the intensity of the CPN pulse to bring the reciprocal inhibition in, which is what the configuration was recorded for.

  4. projectFR – Reciprocal inhibition 1.1 s · projectfr-reciprocal-inhibition

    The two responses one after the other, far apart in time: a 13 mA pulse on the posterior tibial nerve at 2 ms and a 19 mA one on the common peroneal nerve at 1001 ms, with the connectivities and conductances of the projectFR example (100% from the TA afferents to the interneurons, 20% from them to the motoneurons).

    Look at the EMG of the soleus, the spikes of the interneurons and the membrane potential of one motoneuron: the first pulse gives an H reflex, the second an inhibitory potential.

  5. ISB_2013 – Stretch Reflex 30 s · remoto-24-isb-2013-stretch-reflex

    The configuration with everything in place that most of the converted scenarios build on: the four ankle muscles with their motoneuron pools, the Ia afferents of the soleus and of the gastrocnemii exciting all three, and 400 descending axons on the triceps surae. The ankle is isometric.

    It is the longest of the group and the one to copy when you want the whole ankle and nothing missing from the tables. For the stretch itself, see the next one.

  6. Ankle stretch reflex 1.5 s · estiramento-tornozelo

    A perturbation imposed on the joint: at 700 ms the ankle is taken 5° into dorsiflexion in 100 ms, stays there for 100 ms and comes back. The spindles of the triceps surae respond to the stretch, the Ia afferents excite the motoneurons of the soleus and of the gastrocnemii, and their force rises.

    Look at the length of the muscle, the rate of the Ia afference and the force, in that order: it is the reflex loop in three plots. Change the trapezoid (Biomechanical → Joint) or the fusimotor drive (Proprioceptors → Muscle Spindle), which can also vary over time.

  7. Biceps Brachii – Stim Ia 30 s · remoto-19-biceps-brachii-stim-ia

    Repetitive stimulation during a contraction: 10 mA pulses at 5 Hz from 1 s to the end, on a nucleus sustained by 400 descending axons. The name comes from the muscle that was studied; the simulation uses the SOL nucleus of the model, with 800 motoneurons and 320 Ia afferents.

    Look at the EMG, pulse after pulse, and at the force: the reflex response changes while the contraction goes on.

  8. projectFR – Motor units with node-of-Ranvier axons 100 ms · projectfr-node-axons

    Three S motoneurons of the soleus with 20 mA pulses at 10 Hz on the posterior tibial nerve, and the one feature that no other scenario uses: at the stimulus point each axon has a node of Ranvier with the channels of the human axon model of Howells et al. (2012), instead of a plain current threshold. The step is 0.005 ms, twenty times finer than usual.

    Look at the spikes of the three axons from pulse to pulse: refractoriness, supernormality and accommodation come out of the node model. Change the pulse width (Nerve Stimulation) to get the strength–duration curve.

3Recurrent

Recurrent inhibition

Motoneurons and Renshaw cells: the only loop of the model that is entirely inside the spinal cord.

  1. identSOLRenshaw 1.5 s · remoto-44-identsolrenshaw

    The soleus with its 350 Renshaw cells, from the original database: the motoneurons receive 400 descending axons with a mean interval of 6 ms, excite the Renshaw cells and are inhibited back by them.

    Look at the spikes of the Renshaw cells beside those of the motoneurons, and at the force. Compare with the same scenario with the recurrent pathway switched off (Structure → Neural Elements): the force is higher and less smooth without it.

  2. projectFR – MN pool with Renshaw cells 5 s · projectfr-mn-pool-renshaw

    The same loop with the parameters of the projectFR example: 800 S motoneurons, 350 Renshaw cells, 6% connectivity one way and 4% the other, a synapse with 5% depression, and both pathways weakening with the distance along the spinal column.

    Look at the rate of the Renshaw cells against the rate of the motoneurons. Change the declining factors (Biomechanical → Miscellaneous) to see how much the distance between cells matters.

  3. projectFR – Renshaw cells with injected current 100 ms · projectfr-renshaw-injected-current

    350 Renshaw cells on their own, with no motoneurons: a constant current of 5 nA in the soma of each one, on top of their spontaneous firing of 7 Hz.

    Look at the interval histogram of the cells: it is the response of the interneuron model to a steady input, with nothing else around it.

  4. projectFR – Antidromic stimulation of MN and RC 600 ms · projectfr-antidromic-stimulation

    The loop driven backwards: 20 mA pulses at 60 Hz on the posterior tibial nerve, on 5 S motoneurons connected to 2 Renshaw cells with 100% connectivity both ways. The antidromic spikes travel up the axons, activate the Renshaw cells, and the cells inhibit the motoneurons.

    Look at the soma and terminal spike trains of the motoneurons: the collision between the spikes that come from the soma and those that come from the stimulus is explicit in the model.

4Proprioceptors

Proprioceptors and the position task

The spindle, the Golgi tendon organ and the afferent feedback, with the foot free to rotate: the scenarios where the mechanics closes the loop.

  1. Position Task 30 s · remoto-25-position-task

    The foot against a load of 15% of the maximum voluntary torque, held by the triceps surae: 400 descending axons with a mean interval of 10 ms and the Ia afferents of the three muscles exciting the motoneurons.

    Look at the ankle angle over the 30 s and at its variability: the task is to hold a position, and what it costs is visible in the fluctuation of the angle and of the force.

  2. Position Task – IIa 30 s · remoto-26-position-task-iia

    The same task with the group II afferents of the soleus and of the gastrocnemii added (500, 200 and 200). The group II interneurons are off, so the II afferents fire but do not reach the motoneurons.

    Look at the rate of the group II afference against the length of the muscle. To close that pathway, switch the group II interneurons on in Structure → Neural Elements.

  3. Position Task – IIa no Ia 30 s · remoto-27-position-task-iia-no-ia

    The same, without the Ia afferents: only the group II ones are left, and they do not reach the motoneurons either, so the triceps surae holds the position with no afferent feedback at all.

    Compare the angle with that of the two scenarios above: it is the cleanest demonstration in the set of what the Ia loop does for the steadiness of a position.

  4. Position Task – Ib 30 s · remoto-28-position-task-ib

    The same task with the Ib afferents of the Golgi tendon organ (300, 120 and 120), which inhibit the motoneurons of the triceps surae through 350 Ib interneurons: the pathway that reports force, not length.

    Look at the Ib afference against the force of the tendon, and at the spikes of the Ib interneurons.

  5. projectFR – Muscle spindle 20 s · projectfr-muscle-spindle

    The spindle of the tibialis anterior on a slow sinusoidal stretch at 0.25 Hz, with the dynamic and static fusimotor drives at 10 spikes/s. The ankle angle is imposed and the muscle does not contract, so what is left is the receptor on its own.

    Look at the rate of the Ia afference beside the length and the velocity of the fascicle: the dynamic sensitivity of the spindle is the phase lead between them.

  6. projectFR – Closed loop with Ia feedback 5 s · projectfr-isometric-closed-loop

    The soleus with the loop closed: 400 descending axons and the Ia afferents driven by the spindle, with the Hill muscle and the ankle following an imposed sinusoid of 5.7° at 1 Hz, with the fusimotor drives at 32 spikes/s.

    Look at the force against the angle: part of the excitation of the motoneurons now comes from the movement itself. Compare with the Ia afferents switched off.

5Command

Descending command and identification

The statistics of the command and what the nucleus does with it: oscillations, noise and the scenarios recorded for system identification.

  1. JN – Watanabe and Kohn (2015) 15 s · remoto-42-jn-watanabe-and-kohn-2015

    The scenario of the paper on how the spinal cord decodes fast oscillatory commands: the soleus receives 400 descending axons with a mean interval of 15 ms and an excitatory synaptic noise, and from 3 s on the rate of the axons gains a 20 Hz sinusoid amplitude-modulated at 1 Hz.

    Look at the FFT of the force (under Analysis): the 20 Hz of the command and the 1 Hz of its envelope are not in the force in the same proportion, which is the point of the paper.

  2. Identification_SOL_MG 15 s · remoto-34-identification-sol-mg

    The triceps surae with a mean interval of 17 ms and an excitatory synaptic noise; from 2 s to the end, the rate of the axons gains a 20 Hz sinusoid of 20 spikes/s.

    Look at the FFT of the force and of the EMG: how much of the oscillation of the command survives in each one is the transfer the configuration was recorded to measure.

  3. AMmodRenshaw 15 s · remoto-41-ammodrenshaw

    The soleus alone with the same oscillatory command (20 Hz from 3 s on). Despite the name, the Renshaw cells are off in the configuration as it was recorded.

    Change that: switch the Renshaw cells on (Structure → Neural Elements) and compare the FFT of the force with and without recurrent inhibition — which is what the name suggests the author was after.

  4. Identification SOL_MG 1.5 s · remoto-36-identification-sol-mg

    The same configuration as 34 with 1.5 s instead of 15 s. Since the sinusoid of the command only starts at 2 s, here the command is constant: it is the control condition, and it runs in a fraction of the time.

  5. Identification_LG 1.5 s · remoto-35-identification-lg

    The two gastrocnemii without the soleus, with 400 descending axons at a mean interval of 3 ms.

    A small, quick scenario: useful as a starting point when what you want is the gastrocnemii and not the whole triceps surae.

  6. IdentSOL 1.5 s · remoto-38-identsol

    The soleus with 400 descending axons at a mean interval of 12 ms, and a single S motoneuron in the lateral gastrocnemius: enough to follow one unit of a second muscle without paying for the whole pool.

    Look at the properties table of the motoneurons: it is the fastest way to see the parameters a single cell gets.

  7. projectFR – Neural tract 10 s · projectfr-neural-tract

    A descending tract on its own: 400 axons firing in a gamma process of order 1 (that is, exponential intervals) at 83.3 spikes/s, with no motoneurons to receive them.

    Look at the raster and the interval histogram of the tract. It is the scenario to use when what you want to check is the command generator itself.

  8. projectFR – Single neural tract unit 10 s · projectfr-neural-tract-unit

    One single descending axon with a mean interval of 4 ms in a gamma process of order 1.

    Look at the mean, the standard deviation and the coefficient of variation of the intervals, in the interval histogram of the tract: with order 1 the coefficient of variation is 1, and raising the order (field Std) brings it down.

6Posture

Posture and biomechanics

The body, the joint and the Hill muscle: the scenarios where the mechanics is the subject.

  1. projectFR – Postural control 12 s · projectfr-postural-control

    Upright stance as an inverted pendulum over the ankle, controlled by the soleus, the gastrocnemii and the tibialis anterior, with Hill muscles, Ia, II and Ib afferents, group II, reciprocal Ia and Ib interneurons, and 400 descending axons at 50 spikes/s. With the values of this scenario the body stays still for about a second and falls backwards.

    The original example repeated up to 100 trials and recorded the time to fall; here it is one trial. For several, vary the fusimotor drive on the command line: remoto sweep --param settings.gammaDynamic --measure "max(articulacao-angulo)".

  2. projectFR – Ankle impedance 2 s · projectfr-ankle-impedance

    The angle of the ankle imposed as the sum of 200 sinusoids from 0.25 to 50 Hz, with the four muscles responding passively: the torque against the angle is the impedance of the joint.

    Change it into the interesting case: switch the CM_ext tract on in Descending Commands and the impedance is measured during a contraction, which is what it depends on.

  3. projectFR – Hill muscle with imposed angle 1 s · projectfr-hill-muscle

    The Hill muscle of the soleus activated by 800 S motoneurons while the ankle follows a sinusoid of 5.7° at 1 Hz, with no afferents.

    Look at the force, the length and the velocity of the muscle together: it is the force–length–velocity behaviour of the model, with the neural side kept simple on purpose.

7Demyelination

Demyelinating diseases

The two tasks of ReMoto_AIDP with its three subjects: the same experiment with healthy axons, with demyelinated ones and with a conduction block. They are meant to be read in threes.

  1. ReMoto_AIDP – Force task (S0, healthy) 12 s · aidp-force-task-s0

    The soleus and the gastrocnemii, with Hill muscles and the ankle isometric, driven by 400 descending axons at 40 spikes/s for 2 s and 250 spikes/s afterwards, with the Ia afferents responding to the spindle. The motor axons conduct from 44 to 53 m/s, from the S units to the FF ones.

    Look at the force of the triceps surae and the ankle torque over the 10 s of contraction: this is the reference the other two are compared against.

  2. ReMoto_AIDP – Force task (S1, demyelinated) 12 s · aidp-force-task-s1

    The same task with moderate demyelination: the motor axons of the S units now conduct from 5 to 51 m/s and those of the FF ones fall from 52 to 40 m/s, so that the thickest axons become the slowest. The sensory axons are untouched.

    Compare the force with S0: the command is the same, and what changed is only when each spike arrives at the end-plate.

  3. ReMoto_AIDP – Force task (S2, demyelinated with conduction block) 12 s · aidp-force-task-s2

    Severe demyelination: the velocities of S1 and, on top of them, 65% of the axons of each nucleus, counting from the slowest, with their conduction blocked — the motoneuron still fires at the soma, and no spike reaches the muscle.

    Look at the force against the spikes of the motoneurons: the dissociation between what the spinal cord does and what the muscle gets is the signature of the block.

  4. ReMoto_AIDP – Position task (S0, healthy) 12 s · aidp-position-task-s0

    The foot free to rotate from 5° on, loaded with 10% of the maximum voluntary torque, held by the four muscles with their Ia afferents, with the command at 72.5 spikes/s in a gamma process of order 7.

    Look at the ankle angle and its variability: unlike the force task, here the feedback loop is closed through the mechanics.

  5. ReMoto_AIDP – Position task (S1, demyelinated) 12 s · aidp-position-task-s1

    The position task with the conduction velocities of S1.

    Compare the variability of the angle with S0: in a task held by feedback, a delay costs more than in an isometric contraction.

  6. ReMoto_AIDP – Position task (S2, demyelinated with conduction block) 12 s · aidp-position-task-s2

    The position task with the velocities of S1 and the conduction block of 65% of each motor nucleus.

    Look at whether the position is held at all: it is the condition in which the model predicts the functional loss the paper describes.

8Everything

The whole model

  1. Full model 2 s · modelo-completo

    Every element of the model turned on at the same time: the four ankle muscles with S, FR and FF motoneurons; the Ia, II and Ib afferents of each, with spindle and Golgi tendon organ; the reciprocal Ia, Ib and group II interneurons and the Renshaw cells of extensors and flexors; the six descending tracts; the Hill muscle and the ankle as an inverted pendulum; and a 12 mA pulse on the posterior tibial nerve at 1 s.

    It is not an experiment — the rates and the pulse were chosen to make everything fire — but it is the scenario to run once to walk around the whole results screen with no empty plots. Two simulated seconds take a few minutes.
